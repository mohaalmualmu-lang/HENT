# Illustration prompts (Phase 5)

For an external image AI. **Send prompts 1 and 2 first** as a style test. When those two come back, I check them (resolution ≥1600 px long side, plain white background with no fake checkerboard, nothing cut off, no text/letters/numbers anywhere, clinical accuracy against your notes) and either lock the style or send exact “keep this image, change only X” fixes. Then the remaining eight go out.

Every prompt ends with the same style block. Each image goes into the module and card named under it, compressed to WebP with a “What to notice” caption.

---

**STYLE BLOCK (append to every prompt, word for word):**
> Style: one single medical illustration, not a grid, not a collage, not a contact sheet, not multiple variations. Clean modern medical-textbook illustration, soft realistic shading, calm natural colours, crisp edges. Plain pure white background (#FFFFFF), no checkerboard, no transparency pattern, no shadows on the background, no frame. The whole subject fully inside the image with a comfortable margin; nothing cropped or cut off. Absolutely no text, no labels, no letters, no numbers, no arrows with words, no watermark, no signature anywhere in the image. Landscape orientation, at least 1600 pixels on the long side.

### 1. `ill_acute_glaucoma.png` — Module 2, glaucoma card (TEST IMAGE)
A close-up front view of one adult’s right eye during an acute attack of narrow-angle glaucoma, shown beside the same person’s normal left eye so the two can be compared, both eyes in the same single picture as one face crop from brow to cheek. In the affected right eye the cornea looks hazy and cloudy like frosted glass, the white of the eye is red around the coloured iris, and the pupil is mid-sized (neither pinpoint nor fully wide), slightly irregular and oval rather than round. The normal left eye has a clear cornea, white sclera and a small round pupil. The eyelids of the right eye are slightly squinted as if in pain. Must-haves: exactly two eyes; only the right eye affected; cloudy cornea, mid-position dilated irregular pupil and redness in the right eye only.

### 2. `ill_epiglottitis_tripod.png` — Module 6, epiglottitis card (TEST IMAGE)
A full-body side view of a frightened young child, about five years old, sitting upright on the edge of a bed in the classic tripod position: leaning forward, both hands planted on the knees, neck stretched forward and chin pushed out in the sniffing position, mouth slightly open with a small line of drool at the corner of the lips. The child looks unwell and anxious, with pale skin. No adult hands touch the child; no medical instrument is near the mouth. Must-haves: upright posture, both hands on knees, chin forward, visible drool, nothing being put in the child’s mouth.

### 3. `ill_cellulitis_compare.png` — Module 2, periorbital vs orbital cellulitis
One illustration showing two children’s faces side by side, from forehead to chin. Left face: periorbital (preseptal) cellulitis — the eyelids of the right eye are red, swollen and puffy, but the eye itself looks straight ahead normally and the white of the eye is mostly white. Right face: orbital cellulitis — the eyelids are just as swollen and red, and in addition the eyeball bulges slightly forward, the white of the eye is red and swollen, and the eye does not look straight ahead like the other eye. Must-haves: both faces the same age and skin tone; the only differences are the bulging, misaligned, red eyeball on the right face.

### 4. `ill_corneal_abrasion.png` — Module 2, corneal abrasion card
A close-up of one open eye seen from the front under a cobalt-blue examination light, with a single irregular bright green-yellow fluorescent scratch-shaped patch on the clear cornea over the lower half of the iris. The white of the eye is mildly red and there is a tear on the lower lid. Must-haves: one eye only; one scratch-shaped glowing patch on the cornea; no instruments.

### 5. `ill_epistaxis_sagittal.png` — Module 4, epistaxis table
A cutaway side view (sagittal section) of an adult head showing the nasal cavity and throat. Two bleeding sites are shown as small red spots: one at the front of the nasal septum just inside the nostril (anterior), with a few blood drops coming out of the nostril; one far back in the nasal cavity near the throat (posterior), with a thin stream of blood running down the back of the throat toward the oesophagus. The head is upright and slightly tilted forward. Must-haves: exactly two bleeding points; front one drains out of the nostril; back one drains down the throat.

### 6. `ill_ludwig_angina.png` — Module 5, Ludwig angina card
A three-quarter front view of an adult man’s head and neck: the area under the chin and the upper front of the neck is red, firm-looking and swollen, the jaw line is lost in the swelling, the mouth is slightly open with the tongue pushed upward, and a little drool runs from the lip. He sits upright looking uncomfortable. Must-haves: swelling under the chin and front of the neck (both sides), raised tongue, drooling.

### 7. `ill_eardrum_compare.png` — Module 3, otitis card
One illustration with two round otoscope-style views of an eardrum, side by side on white. Left: a normal eardrum — thin, pearly-grey, slightly translucent and concave, with a cone of reflected light. Right: otitis media — the eardrum is red, thick, opaque and bulging outward, with no light reflex. Must-haves: exactly two circular views; only colour, thickness and bulging differ.

### 8. `ill_papilledema_disc.png` — Module 2, papilledema card
One illustration with two round views of the back of the eye (the retina, as seen through an ophthalmoscope), side by side on white. Left: a normal optic disc — a sharp-edged, pale yellow-pink round disc with blood vessels leaving it. Right: papilledema — the same disc swollen, raised and blurred at the edges, with the blood vessels bending over the swollen margin. Must-haves: exactly two circular retina views; only the disc swelling and blurred edges differ.

### 9. `ill_eyedrop_pouch.png` — Module 1, eye-drop sequence
A close-up of a gloved hand gently pulling down a patient’s lower eyelid with one fingertip to make a small pouch, while the other gloved hand holds a small dropper bottle just above the pouch without touching the eye. The patient looks slightly upward. Must-haves: lower lid pulled down to form a pouch; dropper above the pouch, not touching the eye; gloves on.

### 10. `ill_irrigation_direction.png` — Module 1, irrigation card
A front view of a patient lying on their back with the head turned slightly to one side, the affected eye held open by a gloved hand, and a gentle stream of clear fluid poured from a bottle onto the inner corner of the eye (beside the nose) so that the fluid runs across the eye and away toward the outer corner and off the side of the face. Must-haves: the stream starts at the inner corner next to the nose; fluid flows outward away from the nose and away from the other eye; gloves on.

## Review checklist (applied to every image that comes back)
1. One single illustration (reject grids, collages, variations).
2. Long side ≥ 1600 px.
3. Pure white background, no checkerboard or transparency pattern.
4. Nothing cut off at the edges.
5. No text, letters, numbers or label-like marks anywhere.
6. Clinical must-haves present exactly as listed (counts, sides, what differs between panels), and consistent with your notes.
7. Fix requests are written as: “Keep this image. Change only: …”.
